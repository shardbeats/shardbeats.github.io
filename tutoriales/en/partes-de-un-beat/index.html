Beat structure: intro, verse and hook arrangement

If you searched for the parts of a beat and do not know how many bars each section needs, here is the classic intro, verse and hook structure with an example ready for your DAW.

This guide to the parts of a beat is for beatmakers who already make 8-bar loops but cannot turn them into a full song. The structure is not a straitjacket: it is a map so the artist knows when the verse drops in, when the hook explodes and when the song should breathe. With these parts of a beat clear, you can arrange any genre, from boombap to trap.

Step 1: open with intro and verse

The intro (4 bars) presents the beat without revealing everything: come in with just the melody and add one new element per bar. The verse (16 bars) is the longest and steadiest section: this is where the artist raps or sings, so keep full drums but avoid ear candy that fights the vocal.

Step 2: build tension with pre-hook and hook

The pre-hook (2 or 4 bars) builds tension right before the chorus: drop the bass, add a riser or repeat the last melody phrase an octave up. The hook (8 bars) is the biggest moment of the beat and the part the audience must remember: bring in everything, bass, main melody, countermelody and room for ad-libs.

Step 3: close with the outro and the full map

The outro (4 bars) closes the beat by taking apart what you built: pull the drums out in stages and leave the melody alone on the last bar for a clean ending. A classic 60-bar map looks like this: 4-bar intro, 16-bar verse, 4-bar pre-hook, 8-bar hook, 16-bar verse, 8-bar hook and 4-bar outro.

Step 4: how to mark sections in the DAW

In FL Studio, mark sections with markers (Alt+T) or by renaming your patterns per section: INTRO, VERSE, PRE, HOOK and OUTRO. In the Playlist, paint each section a different color and leave a small visual gap between blocks to read the arrangement at a glance. With the parts of a beat marked this way, moving the hook or extending the verse takes seconds.

Step 5: glue it together with simple transitions

Connect the sections with three simple transitions that never fail: a crash or downlifter coming into the hook, half a bar of silence before the drop to build anticipation, and a filter opening up gradually through the pre-hook. FAQ

How many bars does each section need?

As a reference: 4-bar intro, 16-bar verse, 2 or 4-bar pre-hook, 8-bar hook and 4-bar outro. A typical full map adds up to 60 bars: 4-bar intro, 16-bar verse, 4-bar pre-hook, 8-bar hook, 16-bar verse, 8-bar hook and 4-bar outro. Adapt the numbers to the genre.

What is the hook?

The hook is the chorus: the biggest and most memorable section of the beat, usually 8 bars, where every element comes in. It is the part the audience sings along to.

How do I mark sections in FL Studio?

Use markers with Alt+T or rename your patterns as INTRO, VERSE, PRE, HOOK and OUTRO. Then paint each section a different color in the Playlist.
