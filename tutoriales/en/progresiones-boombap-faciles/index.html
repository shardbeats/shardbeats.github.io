Easy boombap chord progressions in 5 steps

Easy boombap chord progressions that sound like a record from the first chord: from tempo to final bounce in 5 steps, with no extra theory.

This guide to easy boombap chord progressions is for beatmakers who open the piano roll and go blank: they want that gospel and soul color of classic boombap, but every chord sounds thin or out of place. A tempo with measured swing, seventh and ninth voicings that always work, three proven loops to start with, and a method for humanizing even if you do not play piano. Work in Cm or C and transpose later.

Step 1: set the tempo between 83 and 95 BPM with swing from 55 to 60%

Set the tempo between 83 and 95 BPM and the swing between 55 and 60%. That range is the boombap pocket: below it sounds sleepy and above it drifts toward another genre. Work in Cm or C and transpose later: composing in a comfortable key lets you focus on the rhythm of the chords, and moving everything one or two semitones at the end gives you free variations of the same loop. Start at 57% and adjust by ear with the drums playing.

Step 2: build 7th and 9th voicings with a maximum of 5 notes

Build voicings of 4 or 5 notes with sevenths and ninths: the left hand plays 3 and 7, the right 9 and 13, with a maximum of 5 notes per instrument so nothing fights in the mix. Your reference is the open Cm9: C2 in the bass plus Eb3-G3-Bb3-D4 on the keys. That gap between the low bass and the chord above leaves air for the kick and the 808. If a chord sounds muddy, remove notes before touching the equalizer.

Step 3: start with one of these 3 loops, one chord per bar

Start with one of these 3 loops, with one chord per bar: gospel I-vi-IV-V (Cmaj9 - Am9 - Fmaj9 - G13), soul ii-V-I (Dm9 - G13 - Cmaj9), and the sweet lift C6/9 - F6/9 - C6/9 - G13. One chord per bar gives each change its weight and leaves room for the vocal to breathe. The gospel I-vi-IV-V is the safest: four chords that are impossible to make sound bad at 85 BPM. Record the four bars on loop and decide by ear which one stays.

Step 4: follow the kick with the piano and add the half-bar G7

Follow the kick with the piano: chop the chord right where the kick hits and keep the organ on long notes underneath. That contrast between the chopped piano and the sustained organ is the classic boombap movement. For soul tension, add a half-bar G7 as a pickup before returning to Cm9: the B natural, outside the key, is the color that raises eyebrows. Use it once every 4 or 8 bars.

Step 5: humanize without playing live and save with BPM_Key naming

Humanize without playing live: record one chord per take with no metronome and let your natural pulse move the timing. If you cannot record that way, apply Randomize from 8 to 12% plus Strum from 10 to 18 ticks, and never leave anything quantized at 100%. Save your progression in Ripchord to reuse it in other beats, and bounce two versions, DRY and WET, with BPM_Key naming (for example 90_Cm).

FAQ

Which progression do I start with?

The gospel I-vi-IV-V: 4 chords, 1 per bar, impossible to make sound bad at 85 BPM.

How many notes per chord?

Maximum 5 per instrument: bass 1 note, left hand 3-7, right hand 9-13.

How do I humanize if I do not play piano?

One chord per take with no metronome; otherwise Randomize 8-12% and Strum 10-18.
