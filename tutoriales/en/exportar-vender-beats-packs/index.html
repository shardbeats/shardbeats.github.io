Export beats to sell in 5 steps

Export beats to sell that pass any store review: from format to final checklist in 5 steps, with no refunds.

This guide to export beats to sell is for beatmakers with good loops that never sell: the WAV arrives over level, the MP3 sounds different from the project and the buyer returns the pack because the names do not even state the BPM. With the exact sale format, levels metered per loop type, mandatory BPM_Key naming, both DRY and WET versions in every folder and a 6-point checklist before bouncing, every pack ships store-ready from the first upload. Include your Demo_Beat and your BPM and Key list.

Step 1: export WAV 44.1kHz 24-bit with Normalize OFF

The sale format is WAV at 44.1kHz and 24-bit, with Normalize OFF and a -1.0dBTP ceiling so no peak touches digital zero. Trim tails in Edison with a 5ms fade: long enough to avoid clicks, short enough not to eat the next chop attack. MP3 is only for the demo the buyer hears before paying, never the product. Bouncing the sale loop with Normalize ON hands your dynamic ceiling to the store algorithm for free.

Step 2: meter levels per type, do not master loops like beats

Meter levels per type and do not master loops as if they were beats: melody loops sit at -12 to -10 LUFS peaking at -1dB; drum loops at -10 to -8 LUFS short-term; and one-shots normalized to -1dB peak with no limiter on top. On the loop mix bus, Tape Machine 24 plus 2dB of bus compression is enough to glue without squashing. An overcompressed loop leaves the buyer no room for their own chain: sell dynamics, not loudness, and let whoever builds the final beat handle the master.

Step 3: name everything BPM_Key_Instrumento_Efecto_DRY-WET

Name everything with the mandatory BPM_Key_Instrumento_Efecto_DRY-WET format, for example 90_Cm_Rhodes_388_DRY: whoever opens the folder knows tempo, key, source and version without playing anything. Organize into folders per category with DRY and WET inside each, plus a Demo_Beat folder with the example beat. A pack full of files called final_final2.wav does not sell twice: naming is half the product when the buyer scans hundreds of loops a day.

Step 4: always sell both versions, DRY and WET

Always sell both versions: DRY with no reverb or delay so the producer can filter and process to taste, and WET with Fame, Sunset or Tape Echo as a reference for the sound you imagined. Boombap wants to filter itself, so the clean DRY is the one that gets worked and the WET is the one that wins hearts in the preview. Every pack ships its Demo_Beat.mp3 plus the BPM and Key list: with no demo the buyer never clicks, and with no key list your pack never shows up when they filter by key.

Step 5: run the 6-point checklist before bouncing

Run the 6-point checklist before bouncing in FL: 20 to 30Hz highpass with EQual, leave chop room with 2 to 4 bars with tail, bounce DRY and WET, name correctly, check in mono with the Stereo Sep knob and meter with Metering. Tag per type to sell with the BPM_Key_Type format: gospel 83 to 88 and classic 88 to 95. This two-minute checklist prevents 90% of refunds: loops with muddy lows, no tail to chop or bad names are the three reasons a pack gets returned.

Name and convert before uploading

FAQ

WAV or MP3 to sell?

WAV 44.1/24 for sale, MP3 demo only. Normalize OFF and -1.0dBTP ceiling.

Why DRY and WET?

Boombap wants to filter itself: clean DRY to work with, WET as sound reference.

Can I sell loops with library one-shots?

Not loose because of redistribution. Yes as part of your composed loop or beat, checking the exact license.
