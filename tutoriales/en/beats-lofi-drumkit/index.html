How to make lo-fi beats

If you search for lo-fi beats with a warm nostalgic sound, here you will make your first lo-fi beat in minutes using a free drumkit and simple techniques.

Lo-fi beats live on imperfection: crackling vinyl, dirty drums and chopped samples like an old cassette. You do not need expensive gear; with a well chosen free drumkit and four texture decisions you have a solid beat. This guide takes you from silence to loop in five steps, with the base video and the free drumkit from the tutorial.

Step 1: vinyl and ambience bed

Open your DAW at 70–85 BPM and lay the vinyl first: needle noise, soft clicks and a little hiss. Find it in your library or record a real vinyl loop and set it to -18 dB, just to feel it. Add a short pad or ambience with reverb so silence is not dry. This bed makes everything on top sound vintage without muddying the mix.

Step 2: dirty drums with the free drumkit

Load the free drumkit from the video: round kicks, mid-weight snares and closed hi-hats with variation. Program 2 bars with intentionally dirty drums: nudge hi-hats 10–15 ticks late, lower velocity to 70–90 and leave a soft ghost snare. Do not quantize to 100%; swing 50–60% is the lo-fi groove key.

Step 3: sample chops that breathe

Import a melodic sample —piano, Rhodes or clean guitar— and make sample chops of one or two bars. Cut by transients and reorder two slices so the progression is not obvious. Pitch down 2–3 semitones, low-pass at 8 kHz and leave a sixteenth silence between chops. That air makes the beat breathe. In FL Studio, chop in Edison or directly in the playlist.

Step 4: texture with saturation, filter and wow

With drums and chops ready, add texture. Run the melodic bus through subtle tape saturation, an automated low-pass opening on the hook and a touch of wow/flutter. Roll off extreme highs and push low-mids for warmth. The warm veil defines lo-fi beats: listen low, if texture is felt without annoyance, it is right.

Step 5: arrange, name and export

Build a simple arrangement: 4-bar intro with vinyl and filtered chops only, verse with full drums, hook with wider texture and filtered outro. Name your beat with TRAKKNAME, the free TRAKK SUITE tool that generates unique names without repeats, and export as WAV at 44.1 kHz. FAQ

Which free drumkit to use for lo-fi beats?

The one in the video is a solid start: round kicks, noisy snares and full hi-hats. Any free drumkit with 16-bit one-shots and some saturation works; avoid kits too clean from modern trap.

How to make drums sound dirty without sounding bad?

Nudge hi-hats slightly off grid, vary velocity and add 55–60% swing. Layer low vinyl noise and light saturation on the drum bus: controlled dirt, not clipping.

Do I need real vinyl for lo-fi beats?

No. A vinyl loop or vinyl plugin with noise and clicks is enough. Keep it low in the mix as texture, not as lead effect, and filter below 40 Hz to keep lows clean.
