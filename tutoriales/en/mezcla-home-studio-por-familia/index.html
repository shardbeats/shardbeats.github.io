Mix by instrument family home studio in 5 steps

Mix by instrument family home studio without frequency fights: every instrument in its place, from piano to final bus, in 5 steps.

This guide to mix by instrument family home studio is for beatmakers whose beat sounds fine solo but turns to mud once everything plays together: the piano covers the bass, the strings fight the vocal and the stereo feels loaded to one side. The problem is almost never owning too few plugins, it is the method: giving each family its fixed place with cuts that always apply, a bass that fills without dirtying, strings that support without getting in the way, a canon panning that never fails and a final glue of reverb and tape. Five steps with exact values, ready to apply in your home studio.

Step 1: piano centered and upfront, organ behind

Place the piano centered and upfront: cut 100-300Hz ALWAYS to leave room for the bass and boost the high-end for a clean breathing top. That low cut is not optional, it is what separates a piano that sings from one that muddies the whole low end. Send the B-3X organ behind: cut 500Hz-1kHz so it never competes with the vocal, set the rotary to slow so it sits still in the mix, and use the slide-up only on transitions, like a push announcing the change that then disappears.

Step 2: bass that fills without mud using MODO P and EQ-81

Bass that fills without mud takes little: MODO P with flatwound playing roots, EQ-81 adding 60Hz for weight and a top cut only if it sounds dull. Nothing else: bass on roots holds the progression without arguing with the kick. And avoid 7.5/250 tape on the bass or it turns to mush, because that combination fattens exactly the zone you just cleaned on the piano. If the bass sounds thin after the piano cut, raise the 60Hz on the EQ-81 before touching anything else.

Step 3: strings one octave up, brass in short hits

Strings that stay out of the vocal use BBC in Legato or Sustain plus Intimate Longs, copying the progression one octave up with long notes. That octave of distance moves them out of the vocal range without removing their presence, and the long notes turn them into a cushion instead of competition. Heads up: the VST already has reverb, so do not drown it with more. And brass goes in short hits, never in lines: a dry stab that answers and goes quiet, not a melody arguing with the singer.

Step 4: canon panning with opposite sides and subtle 10-15

The canon panning is simple: OPPOSITE sides and subtle, between 10 and 15, never full. Place brass right and strings left as call/response: one asks, the other answers, and the ear follows effortlessly. The rule per project is one left, one right and one middle, spreading the families so the center stays free for kick, bass, vocal and snare. Full L/R only works on headphones and collapses on speakers and phones: subtle translates everywhere.

Step 5: final glue with 10-15% plate and starter tape

The final glue joins everything without drowning anything: plate reverb at 10 to 15% wet on strings to give them depth without covering their character, and a short spring on drums to give them air. The starter tape is piano and strings with 99 at 15 ips 456, drums with Oxide 7.5, and the mix bus with 24 at 30 ips 499 staying under 1 to 2dB of reduction. That combination glues the families with analog glue and leaves the bus breathing: if the needle moves more than 2dB, you are crushing the mix instead of gluing it.

FAQ

Does the piano fight the bass?

Cut 100-300Hz on the piano always and leave the low end to the kick and bass.

How much reverb on strings?

10-15% wet: depth without drowning the character.

Full L/R panning?

No: 10-15 subtle, opposite sides, guide as direction and ears per sample.
