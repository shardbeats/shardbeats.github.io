FL Studio stock effects in 5 steps

FL Studio stock effects that sound like real tape and console: from minimal chain to final bounce in 5 steps, buying nothing.

This guide to FL Studio stock effects is for beatmakers who think they sound cheap for not owning RC-20 or Valhalla: they stack paid plugins on the wishlist while their loops keep sounding flat and digital. With the minimal chain of tape, EQ, plate, slap and compression, exact values per track type and substitutes that already open in your FL, every loop comes out with tape color and record space without spending a cent.

Step 1: stick to the minimal chain, nothing else

The minimal chain covers everything: tape plus stock EQ plus plate or spring plus slap or tape delay plus LA-2A or stock compressor. Nothing else. Be intentional: every effect has a job, and if you cannot say what it adds, it stays off. Five well-made decisions sound more expensive than fifteen plugins on default.

Step 2: use tape with judgment, subtle on master and dirty on tracks

Use tape with judgment: on master, subtle, barely gluing; on instruments, to dirty up whatever sounds too digital. Starting points per color: 70s soul with 456 at 15 ips; lofi grit with 250 at 7.5 ips; clean punch with 499; and mix bus with 24 at 30 ips. Tape on the track is heavy makeup and on the master it is sealing dust: pushed hard on both sides, the loop turns to mud.

Step 3: EQ before everything, 20 to 30Hz highpass

EQ before everything: 20 to 30Hz highpass always, on every track with no exceptions, and cut the muddy 100 to 300Hz where boombap mud piles up. For color after cleaning: EQP-1A, MEQ-5 or EQ-81 depending on the track. And in the piano versus bass duel, take bass out of the piano: the piano does not need weight down low if the 808 already lives there.

Step 4: low-key space with 10-15% plate and 80 to 120ms slap

Keep the space low-key: 10 to 15% wet plate reverb on strings to give them depth without drowning, and short spring on drums so they breathe without puddling. Simple delay: 80 to 120ms slap with low feedback on vocals to fatten without dirtying; on guitar you can open up more as occasional ear candy, a wink every 8 bars. Never drown the character: if the reverb shows up before the melody, halve the wet and listen again.

Step 5: compress with presets plus ears, no over-compressing

Compress with presets plus ears, no over-compressing: 2 to 3dB of LA-2A on melodies and vocals to control without stamping; 5 to 7dB of 76-style drums for the hit; and 2dB on the mix bus to glue without squashing. With no Valhalla or RC-20 in sight, the installed substitutes cover the function: RC-20 with TASCAM 388 plus Saturator X; Valhalla with Pure Plate or CSR Plate and CSR Hall; Pro-Q3 with Parametric EQ 2; and EchoBoy with Delay Lab or Fruity Delay 3. They are approximations covering the function, not exact clones.

FAQ

Should I buy RC-20 or Valhalla?

No need to start: TASCAM 388 plus Saturator X covers color; Pure Plate and CSR cover space.

How much reverb?

10-15% wet on strings; short spring on drums. Never drown the character.

How do I dirty up something too clean?

Tape on the instrument, 250 at 7.5 ips to destroy, plus light saturation. Always subtle on master.
